"Same here." She settled into the chair, placing her small purse on the table. "Priya decided to join another excursion with some friends she made. I was wandering around, feeling a bit lost."

"Well, you found me," I said, then immediately felt awkward about how that sounded.

But Sophia's smile widened. "I'm glad I did."

The barista approached our table. "What can I get you today?"

"Coffee?" I asked Sophia.

"Something cold, I think. It's warmer than I expected." She studied the menu board. "Iced coffee with cream?"

"Coming right up."

As the barista walked away, Sophia turned back to me. "So what's the plan now? Are you going to explore on your own?"

I made a decision. Emily was right. It was time to get back in the boat.

"Would you like to explore together?" I asked. "I know a few spots that might be worth seeing."

"You've been here before?"

"Once, during medical school. Spring break trip with friends." I leaned back in my chair. "There's a garden called Par-la-Ville Park that's beautiful this time of year. And if you're interested in history, St. Peter's Church is one of the oldest continuously used Anglican churches in the Western Hemisphere."

Sophia's eyes brightened. "You're full of surprises, Dr. Carter."

"I try to be." I felt more relaxed than I had all morning. "So what do you say? Want to play tourist with me?"

She hesitated for just a moment. I could see her weighing something internal. Professional concerns, maybe. The same ones that had been plaguing me.

Then she nodded. "I'd love to."

The barista returned with Sophia's iced coffee. She took a sip and sighed contentedly.

"Perfect," she said. "Just what I needed."

I watched her face as she savored the drink. There was something about the way she approached simple pleasures that made me want to see more. To discover what else brought that peaceful expression to her features.

*This could be the start of something good,* I thought. *If I'm brave enough to let it happen.*

---

We left the café together, stepping into Hamilton's warm afternoon air. The streets were busy with cruise passengers and locals going about their day. Colorful buildings lined the narrow roads, creating a postcard-perfect scene.

"Which way first?" Sophia asked, adjusting her sunglasses.

"The garden's just a short walk," I said. "We can work our way toward the church from there."

As we walked, our conversation flowed easier than it had since we'd first met. Sophia asked about my previous visit to Bermuda. I found myself sharing stories I hadn't thought about in years. The terrible sunburn Alex had gotten. The local restaurant where we'd eaten fish sandwiches every day.

"You were close to your friends in medical school?" she asked.

"Close enough. Though we've all scattered now. Alex is the only one I still see regularly."

"What about you? Any medical school friendships that survived?"

Sophia was quiet for a moment. "A few. But between residency and research, it's hard to maintain connections. Everyone's so busy building their careers."

There was something wistful in her voice. It made me want to ask more, but we'd reached the entrance to Par-la-Ville Park.

"Here we are," I said, gesturing toward the gate.

The garden was as beautiful as I remembered. Tropical plants and flowers created a lush landscape. Paved paths wound between carefully tended beds. The air smelled of blooming jasmine and salt from the nearby harbor.

Sophia stopped just inside the entrance, taking it all in. "This is wonderful. So peaceful compared to the busy streets."

We walked deeper into the garden. The paths were designed to encourage slow exploration. We found ourselves naturally adjusting our pace, letting the peaceful atmosphere work its magic.

"Tell me something I don't know about you," Sophia said as we paused near a fountain.

The question caught me off guard. "Like what?"

"Anything. Something that might surprise me."

I thought for a moment. "I wanted to be a marine biologist when I was a kid. I was obsessed with sharks."

Sophia laughed. "Really? What changed your mind?"

"My sister got sick." The words came out before I could stop them. "When Emily was diagnosed with lupus, I spent so much time in hospitals. I started paying attention to the doctors, how they interacted with patients and families. I realized I wanted to be the kind of doctor who made people feel heard."

Sophia's expression softened. "That's a beautiful reason to choose medicine."

"What about you? Did you always want to be a researcher?"

"Not exactly. I wanted to be a doctor originally. But during undergraduate work, I fell in love with the lab. The puzzle of it, you know? How to make molecules do what you need them to do."

"And your cousin's condition influenced that choice?"

"Lucia's diagnosis came later. But it definitely shaped my focus." Sophia touched a hibiscus flower as we passed. "There's something powerful about working on treatments for diseases that affect people you love."

We continued walking, the conversation turning to lighter topics. Sophia had a quick wit that surfaced when she felt comfortable. She made observations about the garden, the other visitors, the island itself that made me see things differently.

As we neared the exit, I found myself not wanting the afternoon to end.

"Ready for the church?" I asked.

"Lead the way," she said.

But as we walked toward St. Peter's, I caught her glancing at her watch.

"Do you need to be somewhere?" I asked.

"No, it's just..." She looked uncertain. "I should probably check in with the conference schedule. Make sure I'm not missing anything important."

I nodded, though I felt disappointed. The professional walls were going back up. The same ones I'd been hiding behind for years.

"Of course," I said. "We should probably both check in."

But as we walked, I made another decision. Emily was right about not wasting time on fear. If Sophia needed space to think about professional boundaries, I could respect that. But I wasn't going to disappear again.
