# Chapter 16: Second Chances
## Nathan

"You're running away!" Emily's voice crackled through my phone. Her accusation hit closer to home than I wanted to admit.

I shifted in my seat at Rock Island Coffee. Through the window, Hamilton's colorful storefronts painted a bright backdrop. "I'm not running away. The tour was canceled."

"Instead of finding her, you're hiding in a coffee shop. Classic Nathan avoidance technique."

"I'm not avoiding anything," I protested. The coffee cooling untouched before me suggested otherwise. "I'm enjoying some quiet time before exploring the island."

"Right." Emily's tone dripped skepticism. "You kissed her last night. Now you're pretending it didn't happen?"

I sighed, rubbing my temple. Of course Alex had told Mari, who'd told Emily. The gossip chain was nothing if not efficient.

"It's complicated, Em."

"Only because you make it complicated. Normal people kiss someone they like, then spend more time with them. They don't analyze it until all joy is gone."

"She's a pharmaceutical researcher," I reminded her, lowering my voice. The café was nearly empty, but still. "I'm a physician who could influence prescribing patterns for her company's drugs."

"Oh please." Emily's eye roll was audible. "You're both professionals. You know how to maintain ethical boundaries."

"That's not the only issue." I stared into my coffee. "After Vanessa..."

"It's been five years, Nathan."

"I know how long it's been," I replied sharply, then softened my tone. "Sorry. I just don't want to repeat past mistakes."

A brief silence fell between us. Emily had witnessed my slow recovery after Vanessa left. She'd watched as I retreated into work and built walls around myself.

"Sophia isn't Vanessa," Emily said finally, her voice gentle. "From everything you've told me, she sounds nothing like her."

That much was true. Where Vanessa had been calculating ambition wrapped in polished charm, Sophia was genuine passion tempered by gentle humility. Where Vanessa had viewed patients as stepping stones to publications, Sophia saw the people behind the disease.

"How are you feeling?" I asked, changing the subject. "Any improvement with the new medication?"

"Don't deflect. The joint pain is still bad. I had to cancel my afternoon classes yesterday."

Worry tightened my chest. "Have you called Dr. Kelley?"

"My appointment's tomorrow. Before you start micro-managing my condition, let's get back to your love life. It's more interesting than my swollen joints."

I snorted. "There is no love life to discuss."

"Not with that attitude." Emily's voice turned serious. "Nathan, you deserve to be happy. Not just professionally satisfied, but happy."

"I am happy," I insisted weakly.

"No, you're comfortable. There's a difference." Her sigh carried through the line. "Remember when we were kids and Dad would take us sailing on the reservoir?"

"Of course."

"You loved it until the accident. After you fell overboard that summer, you never went back on a boat. Not once. Sailing had been your favorite thing."

"What's your point?"

"You're still afraid of falling. Maybe it's time to get back in the boat. Not all relationships end with you drowning."

My throat tightened. "Poetic, but..."

"No, listen to me. I live with daily reminders that life is fragile. Pain has taught me not to waste time on fear when joy is possible."

The weight of her words settled over me. Emily had faced more health challenges by twenty-eight than most people encounter in a lifetime. Yet she approached life with more openness than I'd managed in years.

"What if I've forgotten how?" I asked quietly. "How to let someone in?"

"You haven't," she said with confidence. "You're rusty, not broken. Call her, Nathan. Spend the day together. See what happens without analyzing it to death first."

"The walking tour was canceled. I don't even know where she is."

"Then call Mari and get Sophia's number. Or better yet, text Alex to find out from Priya. They're all conspiring anyway. Might as well use it to your advantage."

I laughed despite myself. "When did my little sister become so wise?"

"Around the same time my big brother became so stubborn. Now go find her."

"I will. And Em? Thanks."

"What are sisters for if not forcing emotional growth? Love you."

"Love you too."

After hanging up, I sat with my thoughts. I turned Emily's words over in my mind. She was right about my pattern of avoidance. Since Vanessa left, I'd constructed a life built around professional excellence and emotional safety. I avoided any relationship that threatened that careful balance.

The memory of Vanessa standing in our bedroom still surfaced sometimes. Suitcases packed. "You're too content with being average," she'd said. Her tone was clinical, as if diagnosing a patient. "I need someone with real ambition, someone who understands what it takes to be exceptional."

Her words had cut deep. Not because they were true, but because they showed how little she'd understood me. My focus on patient care rather than prestigious publications wasn't lack of ambition. It was a different kind of purpose.

I wasn't average, I reminded myself. In five years, I'd built a practice that patients trusted. I'd earned respect from colleagues who valued my judgment. I'd become the physician I'd always wanted to be.

But I'd also become someone who lived alone. Someone who came home to an empty apartment every night. Someone who hadn't held hands with a woman in longer than I cared to admit.

The café door chimed. I looked up, expecting another tourist seeking escape from Hamilton's afternoon heat.

Instead, Sophia walked in.

Her hair was pulled back in a loose ponytail. A few strands had escaped to frame her face. She wore a simple sundress in deep blue that made her skin glow. When she spotted me, her face lit up with a smile that made my chest feel tight.

Joy is possible, Emily's words echoed in my mind.

"Nathan!" Sophia approached my table, looking genuinely pleased to see me. "I thought you'd already started exploring."

"Change of plans," I said, standing to pull out a chair for her. "The tour was canceled too. Low registration."

"Same here." She settled into the chair, placing her small purse on the table. "Priya decided to join another excursion with some friends she made. I was wandering around, feeling a bit lost."

"Well, you found me," I said, then immediately felt awkward about how that sounded.

But Sophia's smile widened. "I'm glad I did."

The barista approached our table. "What can I get you today?"

"Coffee?" I asked Sophia.

"Something cold, I think. It's warmer than I expected." She studied the menu board. "Iced coffee with cream?"

"Coming right up."

As the barista walked away, Sophia turned back to me. "So what's the plan now? Are you going to explore on your own?"

I made a decision. Emily was right. It was time to get back in the boat.

"Would you like to explore together?" I asked. "I know a few spots that might be worth seeing."

"You've been here before?"

"Once, during medical school. Spring break trip with friends." I leaned back in my chair. "There's a garden called Par-la-Ville Park that's beautiful this time of year. And if you're interested in history, St. Peter's Church is one of the oldest continuously used Anglican churches in the Western Hemisphere."

Sophia's eyes brightened. "You're full of surprises, Dr. Carter."

"I try to be." I felt more relaxed than I had all morning. "So what do you say? Want to play tourist with me?"

She hesitated for just a moment. I could see her weighing something internal. Professional concerns, maybe. The same ones that had been plaguing me.

Then she nodded. "I'd love to."

The barista returned with Sophia's iced coffee. She took a sip and sighed contentedly.

"Perfect," she said. "Just what I needed."

I watched her face as she savored the drink. There was something about the way she approached simple pleasures that made me want to see more. To discover what else brought that peaceful expression to her features.

This could be the start of something good, I thought. If I'm brave enough to let it happen.
