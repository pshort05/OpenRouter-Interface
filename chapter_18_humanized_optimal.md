# Chapter 18: Meet the Family (K-Pop Edition)
## Bryan
**Sunday afternoon, late August**

*Now Playing: "My Universe" - Coldplay & BTS*

---

The Uber driver's GPS announced we'd arrived at our destination, but I was too busy staring at the modest two-story house to process the words right away. My palms went slick against my khakis. That familiar finger-tapping rhythm started up against my leg.

*Don't mess this up, Gardner.*

"You're doing the drummer-boy finger thing again," Aileen said, reaching over to still my hand with hers. She looked perfect in her leather jacket over a simple black dress—that kind of easy cool that made me wonder what she was doing with someone who owned three identical polo shirts.

"I'm not—okay, I'm lying. I'm terrified. What if they hate me? What if your dad goes full 'state your intentions'? What if I accidentally insult Korean culture, or call manhwa manga and trigger an international incident?"

"Oh, hell no. Call manhwa 'manga' in front of Moira and she'll weaponize a lightstick." Aileen laughed, squeezing my hand. "But my parents will like you. Be you—the guy who learns K-pop choreo for fun and makes encryption sound like poetry."

"That doesn't read as parent-friendly. Wait, are you being sarcastic? I can't tell."

"That's exactly my parents' flavor."

The house sat on a tree-lined street in Bayside, Queens. The front yard was immaculate, with flower beds arranged in careful patterns. Each plant positioned just so.

We walked up the front steps. Aileen's shoulders straightened with each step, her chin lifting slightly.

"Ready?" she asked, hand on the doorknob.

Before I could answer, the door swung open. A woman who shared Aileen's bone structure stood there, though her voice carried rolling vowels and musical cadences that transported you to distant green hills.

"Eily, love!" Her mother pulled her into a fierce hug, then turned those same bright eyes on me. "And you must be Bryan. I'm Siobhan Kim, though everyone calls me Shiv. Come here, give us a hug."

"Mom, let him breathe before he crosses the threshold," Aileen protested, but the corners of her mouth turned up.

"Nonsense. He's far too skinny. We'll put a proper dinner in him." Siobhan's accent rolled thick as honey. "Patrick! They're here!"

A man appeared from a home office—same sharp eyes as Aileen, same confident posture, but with graying temples and the kind of handshake that came from years of closing deals.

"Bryan Gardner," he said, extending his hand. "Patrick Kim. Welcome to our home."

"Thank you for having me, Mr. Kim—your home is beautiful."

"Please, call me Patrick. And thank Shiv for the house looking good. I'm usually buried in server configurations and security protocols."

My ears perked up. "You're in IT?"

"Director of Information Technology at Morrison Financial. Been there fifteen years now." His expression shifted, eyes focusing more intently. "Eily says you're in data security yourself?"

"Yes, sir. Mostly AI ethics and privacy—keeping user data where it belongs."

"Finally—someone who knows security isn't just walls; it's trust."

"Patrick, don't start talking shop before the boy's even had something to drink," Siobhan called from the kitchen.

"So that's where Aileen gets her security instincts—the threat-modeling apple didn't fall far," I observed.

"Truth be told, she's better. Always has been. As a kid, she'd spot a scam email across the room."

"Dad," Aileen protested, but color rose in her cheeks.

We moved into the living room. Family photos lined the mantelpiece, showing the Kim family evolution from young parents with a toddler to their current incarnation. Several pictures showed a younger Aileen with dark hair streaked with blue, arms crossed in band t-shirts.

"Those were her rebellious years," Siobhan said, following my gaze.

"They were my authentic years, Mom."

"Same thing, love."

Music drifted down from upstairs—K-pop, definitely, but muffled by the ceiling and headphones.

"That'll be Moira," Patrick said. "She's been buzzing to meet you since Eily said you actually listen to Korean music."

"I was thinking I could flex my Korean a little," I admitted, then caught myself. "What little I've got. Rosetta Stone, lyric-level stuff, but..."

Patrick's shoulders dropped slightly. His gaze drifted to a spot on the wall.

"I wish I'd kept my Korean," he said, voice dropping. "My parents used it at home, and I was so intent on being American I let it go. Now I can barely remember the lullabies my mother sang."

The air in the room shifted, growing heavier.

"It's not too late," I offered. "I started from zero; I'm inching along. Maybe we could learn together?"

Patrick's mouth curved upward. "I'd like that."

"EILY! IS THAT BRYAN? THE ONE WHO ACTUALLY KNOWS K-POP?" The voice came from upstairs.

"Yes, Moira. Get down here!" Aileen called back, laughter threading through her words.

Footsteps thundered down the stairs. A younger, more kinetic version of Aileen appeared—same dark hair but with streaks of pink, same sharp eyes but wider, more searching. Her headphones hung around her neck, NewJeans bleeding faintly from the speakers.

"Oh my god, hi, bestie!" Moira practically vibrated. "Unnie said you actually know K-pop. Like, not 'Gangnam Style' know—actually know. No cap."

"I... yeah." I glanced at Aileen, who nodded. "My college roommate pulled me in. It snowballed."

"What's playing, bestie?" Moira asked.

"You can hear it through my headphones?"

"'Get Up' by NewJeans? First track, right? No cap?"

"OH MY GOD." Moira spun toward her family. "He actually knows, bestie. Not just 'oh yeah, Gangnam Style'—he knows knows."

"Moira. Breathe," Aileen said, but her lips pressed together, fighting a smile.

"Can you do any choreo, bestie?" Moira asked.

"Some. I watch the practice videos and, um... I know a few. The mirrored versions, mostly."

"Show me, please! Like, now-now!"

"Moira—" Patrick started, but I was already pulling out my phone.

"If you want a challenge," I said, scrolling through my saved videos, "have you learned 'Thunderous' yet?"

"Stray Kids? That's, like, grad-level choreo—big boss energy. Bestie, okay, let's go."

"Want to learn it?"

I stood in the Kim family living room, demonstrating Changbin's part to a twenty-two-year-old college student. Moira picked up the footwork faster than I'd expected, her movements sharp and exact.

"No—see your hands? Sharper here," I demonstrated. "It's not just the move; it's the attitude."

"Like this?" Moira copied the motion, adding her own flair.

"Perfect. Now, the transition into the chorus—"

Aileen watched from the doorway to the kitchen, her expression soft. Eyes tracking my movements with an unreadable emotion flickering across her face. When our eyes met, warmth spread across her features, and my chest constricted.

"Bryan, you're a natural teacher," Siobhan said, appearing beside Aileen with cups of tea. Ceramic clinked softly against the coffee table. "Moira's been at those moves for weeks."

"He does this thing—makes hard stuff feel easy. Clean execution," Aileen said. The admiration in her voice made me stumble slightly.

"Eily says you met at a conference," Patrick said, settling into his chair. Leather creaked. "Work romance, then?"

"Sort of," I said, pausing the lesson. "I opened by insulting her entire profession in front of five hundred people, which is... yeah. Not my best commit."

"Bryan!" Moira gasped. "Bestie!"

"I got nervous—catastrophically," I explained. "She was brilliant and terrifyingly beautiful, and I panicked. Said something idiotic. Took months to dig out."

"And how'd you make it right, then?" Siobhan asked, leaning forward slightly.

"Mostly groveling. Fancy dinner. Repeated apologies—iterated until she agreed to a second shot."

"Smart," Patrick nodded. "When you find someone worth fighting for, you fight."

"Dad," Aileen protested. Pink crept up her neck.

"What? It's true. Your mother made me wait three dates before she'd even hold my hand."

"Patrick Kim, I did no such thing!"

"Three dates, Siobhan. Documented."

"That's called standards!"

Aileen's parents volleyed, leaning toward each other even while sparring. Their hands found each other on the couch between them.

"Come on," Aileen said, taking my hand. "Before Moira conscripts you for every dance ever choreographed, let me show you the rest of the house."

"I heard that! Unnie, don't steal my trainee!" Moira called as we headed toward the kitchen.

Copper pots hung from a rack above the stove. Herbs grew on the windowsill—basil and rosemary reaching toward the afternoon sun. A pot simmered on the stove, its scent of garlic and thyme perfuming the air. My stomach contracted.

"Your family's wonderful," I told Aileen as she showed me the backyard through the kitchen window.

"They like you. A lot." Her voice carried weight beyond casual observation.

"Yeah?"

"Moira just texted me: 'Keep this one, unnie. He gets it.'"

"Gets what?"

"That what you love doesn't have to make sense to anyone else. Just you."

Before I could respond, Patrick appeared in the doorway.

"Eily, your mother needs a taster. Says it's a matter of Irish honor." He turned to me. "Mind if I borrow Bryan a minute?"

"Be nice, Dad. Seriously."

"I'm always nice."

Aileen snorted but headed toward her mother's voice.

Patrick gestured for me to follow him to his home office. Multiple monitors arranged in an exact arc. Cables routed through management systems that bordered on art. Reference books spine-out in chronological order by publication date.

"Impressive setup—your cable management is immaculate," I said.

"Twenty-five years will make you tidy." Patrick settled behind his desk, fingers steepling. "Can I ask you something, Bryan?"

My pulse quickened.

"Of course."

"What are your plans with my daughter?"

I took a breath. "I care about her. More than I ever have about anyone. Actually, maybe that's not... I love who I am with her. It's early, but... I haven't told her yet—I was waiting for the right moment—but I think I'm falling in love with her. She makes me want to be better."

"Good start. What I'm really asking is whether you're prepared for what being with Eily means."

"I'm not sure I follow."

"She's brilliant. Fierce. Ambitious. She won't be anyone's accessory. She'll build things. Lead. Outshine. Can you handle being with someone who might be more successful than you?"

"I hope she is. The world needs her in rooms with power. If I can help, I will."

"Good answer." Patrick's mouth curved. "She's dated before. Fleeting things. No one she brought home. No one who understood the engine." He paused. "You're the first she's brought home in twenty-six years."

"I'm the first?"

"The first she deemed worth a family introduction. That tells me she sees something real. I hope you do, too."

"I do. Absolutely."

"Then I'm glad she found you."

Before I could process that, Moira's voice carried from the living room.

"BRYAN! Oppa, do you know 'God's Menu'?"

Patrick laughed. "You'd better go. Once she gets a choreo in her head, there's no peace till it's done."

I walked back into the living room where Moira had her phone out, YouTube already loaded.

"I found a tutorial, but bestie, I want to see how you do it first," she explained.

"It's pretty complicated... Actually, it's doable if we break it down," I started, but pulled up my own saved video.

As the opening beats of "God's Menu" filled the room, muscle memory took over. The routine demanded athletic coordination—the kind that had taken weeks to master alone in my apartment. I broke down each section, hands cutting through air in sharp angles. Feet pivoting and sliding across the hardwood.

Just as I was demonstrating the complex chorus choreography, footsteps approached. Aileen walked into the room with her mother behind her, both wearing matching expressions. Watching me teach her sister dance moves in their family living room. Fitting into their Sunday afternoon like I'd been there all along.
